\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon manipulation requires past interactions to guide the action needed now.
We introduce \method{} (\textbf{W}orld--\textbf{A}ction--\textbf{R}etrospection \textbf{M}odel), which uses ongoing interaction as evidence for selectively reusing past behavior.
Situated recollection grounds a candidate-independent transition requirement, while a cross-episode sensorimotor repertoire supplies action--effect events.
We adapt retrieved actions to the current context, compare their predicted consequences with the requirement, and jointly regulate their influence on stochastic initialization and conditioning for flow-based action generation.
\method{} achieves 98.7\% success on LIBERO. On RMBench, our task-specialist models achieve 83.9\% overall and 87.3\% on tasks requiring multiple past observations, versus MemoryWAM's 81.5\%.
Controlled studies connect candidate-specific prediction to improved selection, and adaptive utility improves success under similar-event distraction by 11.0 percentage points over a matched constant-utility control.
Physical destination- and order-memory tasks further demonstrate familiar manipulation guided by episode-specific evidence.
Our results demonstrate selective reuse in tabletop manipulation.
\end{abstract}
