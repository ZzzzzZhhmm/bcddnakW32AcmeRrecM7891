\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduce \method{} for event-grounded retrospection: episode evidence grounds the required transition, predicted consequences guide recalled-action selection, and a shared gate regulates the proposal in both initialization and conditioning.
Our evidence connects these decisions across independently executed candidates, controlled generative interventions, and complete-task outcomes.
The resulting policy improves history-dependent manipulation and preserves useful reuse under misleading experience, with physical destination and order tasks making the role of episode evidence concrete.
Together, these results establish current interaction evidence as a practical basis for selecting and shaping the generative influence of familiar behavior in tabletop manipulation.
