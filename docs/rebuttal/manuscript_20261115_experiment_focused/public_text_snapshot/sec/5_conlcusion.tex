\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\vspace{-0.1cm}
We introduce \method{} for event-grounded retrospection, using current-episode evidence to guide selective reuse of past behavior.
From current context and situated recollection, we infer a candidate-independent transition requirement and compare it with the predicted effects of adapted repertoire actions.
Consequence-gated source transport then jointly regulates stochastic initialization and candidate-derived conditioning, making recalled behavior a revisable prior for action generation.
Experiments on LIBERO and RMBench support the complementary roles of recollection and repertoire; complete-event distraction tests show how explicit consequence comparison preserves useful reuse.
These findings support a design principle for embodied control: current evidence should govern both the selection of past behavior and its influence on action generation.
